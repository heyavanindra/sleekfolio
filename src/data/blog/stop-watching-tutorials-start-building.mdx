Back when I started my development journey, I remember watching endless tutorials.

I'd add hours and hours of lectures to my watchlist (which I never finished).

And at the end of the day, I was still where I started. I hadn't moved a single bit.

Still confused about what was what.

I never had the confidence to build anything on my own. I used to think building an end-to-end application was a tedious task.

This is a situation most beginners find themselves in when they start. They try to collect every possible resource but never finish them. And even if they do, they struggle to retain what they've learned.

At some point, I started realizing that maybe the problem wasn't the resources.

There are endless resources out there, most of them completely free.

The problem was how I approached learning.

I was just waiting to learn everything before building anything.

But here's the thing: there's always something new to learn in software development. You can spend months learning one technology and still find dozens of things you don't know.

I wanted to understand React, databases, Express, Go, authentication, deployment, etc., before I built my first project.

But eventually, here's what I understood.

Instead of adding another tutorial to my watchlist, I started building things.

Just small things. They don't have to be big.

Learning React? Start with a simple component, like an accordion, or even a button that does something when you click it.

Once I stopped relying on tutorials and started building on my own, learning became so much more fun and effective.

A simple accordion project can teach you a lot about React that hours of passively watching tutorials might not.

**1 hour of building vs. 10 hours of watching.**

Building, breaking things, and then fixing them is the best and fastest way I've found to learn anything. And that's what I practice whenever I need to learn something new.

You don't need another tutorial.

You don't need to learn everything.

You just need to build.

It doesn't have to be big. Even building a small component of a bigger application can do wonders.

**Happy learning, folks!**
